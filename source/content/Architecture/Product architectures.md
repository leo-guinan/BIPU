# Product architectures

The product architectures are different answers to the same question:

> What would make people and networks more capable without making the boundary of the system disappear?

They are not one platform disguised as four products.

- **HumAIn OmarchyPi** puts a bounded local node at the edge.
- **Phoneless** protects human attention by delegating routine communication under explicit rules.
- **The Hitchhiker’s Guide to the Future** makes access to personal thinking a governed network relationship.
- **Idea Nexus Ventures** provides the public product and research layer that connects these experiments to humanpower, organizational change, and market intelligence.

Each architecture names its authority boundary, its receipt, and the failure that would make it change.

## Product notes

- [[Architecture/HumAIn OmarchyPi]] — a Raspberry Pi edge node with local context, typed health, and explicit enrollment.
- [[Architecture/Phoneless]] — attention preservation through expected-call routing, verification, escalation, and pilot evidence.
- [[Architecture/The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Future]] — private diaries, public guides, memberships, node passports, and writer-controlled semantic search.
- [[Architecture/INV site architecture and Chroma]] — the static Idea Nexus site, Marvin, and its provenance-backed Chroma index.

## The shared pattern

The systems share a small number of commitments:

1. Start with a human capability or burden, not a model feature.
2. Keep canonical sources separate from derived indexes and generated summaries.
3. Treat guesses as guesses and permissions as server-side decisions.
4. Make opt-in, revocation, and fallback behavior visible.
5. Record an artifact before making a claim about the system.
6. Keep a falsifier close enough to the thesis that it can still hurt.

This is architecture as accountability. The diagram is only the part that fits on the screen.
